## Why it matters to me

## Claude Summary
The most substantive rethinking of modularity in this space. The argument is that LLM coders and their limitations force a reevaluation of how software is structured: today's software is "illegible" — no direct correspondence between code and observed behavior — and insufficiently modular, which breaks three requirements of robust coding: incrementality (small localized changes), integrity (not breaking prior increments), and transparency. The proposed unit is concepts (independent services with own state and actions) composed by declarative synchronizations. The claim relevant to you: because synchronizations are explicit and declarative, they can be analyzed, verified, and generated by an LLM. Jackson's stronger position, stated publicly, is that modularity will have a renaissance precisely because AI forces it — by limiting the LLM's context to a single concept you prevent it breaking unrelated functionality, and token costs drop as context shrinks. He gave two talks in January 2026 ("Modular & Legible Software for AI (& Human) Coders", and one on an LLM-heavy capstone class).
