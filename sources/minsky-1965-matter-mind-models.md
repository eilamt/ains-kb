## Why it matters to me

## Notes

this is where mind theory of computing originated. what of it is relevant to LLMs? this is the main question I am interested in.

It is really interesting. First off the era: the sixties where ideas about intelligent machines started to emerge. Secondly what Minskey is trying to do. Basically he is trying to create a unified explanation or model of both humans and machines. 

He notes that machines such as Turing machines are perfectly capable of "self reflection" in the sense that they can perform computation, but also describe the was they perform computation -- he calls it interpretability, in fact they can spit out a complete description of their operations. 

However when trying to fit this model to humans he tackled a problem - free will. He says that just treating it is stochastic process does not 'feel' right - therefore we are confused. 

Then he predicts that the same will be true with regard to any intelligent machine that self-reflects .. that this problem will arise. 

So Minskey thinks there is no essential difference between humans and future intelligent machines. Maybe we are confused about the nature of free will, but it is not an indicator of anything different than a machine.
